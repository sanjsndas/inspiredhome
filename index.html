    Common Questions

    Interior design involves the planning and construction of interior spaces—altering walls, specifying built-in elements, coordinating structural changes, and addressing how a space functions at the architectural level. Interior decoration, by contrast, concerns the selection and arrangement of finishes, furniture, and accessories within an existing spatial framework. A designer may do both, but the distinction matters when you are deciding who to hire for a renovation versus a refresh.

    Three signs: you find yourself walking through one activity zone to reach another (through the dining table to get to the kitchen), doors open into each other or into furniture, and there is no clear path between primary functions without weaving. Good circulation should be intuitive and near-invisible. If you have to think about how to move through a room, the plan needs revision.

    Ambient lighting provides the baseline—general illumination that makes a room usable. Task lighting makes specific activities possible (reading, cooking, working). Accent lighting creates hierarchy and depth. The most important layer depends on the room: kitchens live on task lighting; living rooms depend on accent and ambient balance. The mistake is relying on a single overhead fixture for all three.

    If the renovation involves structural changes, significant spatial reconfiguration, or regulatory approvals, an architect's involvement typically pays for itself in avoided mistakes and optimized outcomes. For cosmetic updates or furniture-level changes, a designer or skilled contractor may be sufficient. The threshold is usually: if walls are moving, you need an architect.

    Prioritize materials that develop character rather than degrade: solid timber over veneer, stone over laminate, brass and bronze over coated metals, lime plaster over acrylic paint. The test is not how it looks on installation day but how it looks after five years of use. Natural materials with visible grain, patina, and imperfection generally age more gracefully than synthetic materials engineered to look pristine indefinitely.
